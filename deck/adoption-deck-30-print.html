A DECK OF WRITTEN ANSWERS

The Adoption Deck

30 cards for partners and principals asking how their firm should adopt AI.
Can we do AI? — capability and current state
Should we do AI? — opportunity and risk
How would we do it? — implementation orientation
Are we ready? — readiness check
What's the value? — business case framing
AI can do the work. Your firm is paid for the judgement.
START HEREMOVE00 / 30

Write, don't think

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Reflection feels done when it's been thought about. Thought answers hide gaps; written answers expose them.

THE MOVE

Thirty to sixty minutes, pen in hand, one answer per card, in the firm's own words. Alone first, then with the partners.

Written answers expose gaps that mental ones do not.

Can we do AI?AUDIT01 / 30

The tool list

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Personal accounts feel invisible, so the firm's real tool count is higher than the paid one — and the invisible tools are the unmanaged ones.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Every AI tool the firm currently pays for, by name. Plus the personal accounts staff use for work. If you cannot list these confidently, write that down instead.

If you cannot list them, that is the answer.

Can we do AI?AUDIT02 / 30

Three tools, named uses

WHY IT GETS DODGED

“People use it sometimes” feels like an answer. It names neither the work nor the frequency, so it can be neither managed nor improved.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Take three tools. For each: who uses it, how often, and what for. Specific: “Sarah uses ChatGPT to redraft client replies, two or three times a week.”

A use you can name is a use you can govern.

Can we do AI?AUDIT03 / 30

The three groups

WHY IT GETS DODGED

It's easier to sort staff into “uses it” and “doesn't” and stop there. The third group — usually the biggest — is where the unexamined risk sits.

WRITE THIS DOWN

The staff you'd call habitual, capable AI users, by name. The staff who don't use AI at all. If both groups are smaller than headcount: what is the third group doing?

Count the middle group. It's the biggest one.

Can we do AI?AUDIT04 / 30

The unseen use

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Nobody wants to write down where they think the firm is exposed. But shadow use that goes unnamed also goes ungoverned.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Where you think AI is being used in the firm that you do not currently see. Almost every firm has an answer.

Almost every firm has an answer. The answer matters.

Can we do AI?AUDIT05 / 30

Sustained or tolerated?

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Firms conflate tolerating AI use with supporting it. Tolerated use depends on individual initiative and dies with it.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Could the firm sustain AI use it considered important — training, guidance, an internal owner? Or would such use depend on individual initiative?

Supported use survives the person. Tolerated use doesn't.

Can we do AI?MOVE06 / 30

Name the internal owner

WHY IT GOES WRONG

When nobody owns the question “is this appropriate?”, every staff member answers it alone, and the firm's position is whatever the boldest person decided.

THE MOVE

Name the one person staff would naturally go to with “is this appropriate?” — then tell the firm you named them.

A question with no owner gets answered in private.

Should we do AI?AUDIT07 / 30

Where value is plausible

WHY IT GETS DODGED

“Drafting” is where the thinking stops. Generic answers produce generic adoption, and generic adoption produces nothing defensible.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Where would AI most plausibly add value if used well? Be specific: not “drafting” but “first-pass drafts of standard property contract clauses where the senior associate's review remains the binding step.”

Not “drafting”. Which drafts, reviewed by whom.

Should we do AI?AUDIT08 / 30

Where risk exceeds value

WHY IT GETS DODGED

“Data leakage” is abstract, so it gets filed under IT and forgotten. The real risks are concrete and sit in named workflows.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Where would AI introduce risks that exceed the value? Same specificity: “the property associates pasting client emails into ChatGPT to draft replies.”

Name the workflow, not the category.

Should we do AI?AUDIT09 / 30

The sensitive material

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Week by week, sensitive material passes through tools without anyone tracking it, because tracking was never anyone's job.

WRITE THIS DOWN

The most sensitive material your team handles in a week. A month. A year. For each: can you say with confidence whether it has, or has not, passed through any AI tool?

“I don't know” is a finding. Write it down.

Should we do AI?AUDIT10 / 30

The regulator question

WHY IT GETS DODGED

The answer exists in pieces across partners' heads. It has never been written in one place, so it has never been tested.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Your regulator asks tomorrow how the firm uses AI. The answer, in plain English, three or four sentences. Read it back. Would it satisfy you, in their position?

Write the answer before you need it.

Should we do AI?AUDIT11 / 30

The client conversation

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Clients raising AI feels hypothetical until it arrives, and the first conversation should not be improvised.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Has any client raised AI — asking you to use it, not to use it, or how you handle their material? What did you tell them? If not: do you expect it within twelve months?

The first client to ask deserves a rehearsed answer.

Should we do AI?MOVE12 / 30

One page, one position

WHY IT GOES WRONG

The firm's position stays verbal, so it shifts with whoever is asked — and the written form arrives only after an incident.

THE MOVE

Take the regulator answer and make it the firm's one-page AI position: what staff may do, what needs asking first, who owns the question. Date it. Review twice a year.

If it isn't written, it isn't a position.

How would we do it?AUDIT13 / 30

Training that changed something

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Training gets remembered as an event, not measured as a change. If nothing changed afterwards, the training was entertainment.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Any AI training the firm has tried, formal or informal. Who attended? What changed afterwards?

What changed afterwards is the only part that counts.

How would we do it?AUDIT14 / 30

The policy that exists

WHY IT GETS DODGED

A one-page memo from two years ago feels like a policy. If staff can't say what it says, it's a document, not a policy.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Any AI use policy or internal guideline, even one page. Where does it live, when was it last updated, and what does staff actually do in light of it?

A policy nobody operates is a wish with a date on it.

How would we do it?AUDIT15 / 30

The first move

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Every option argues for itself — workshop, policy, infrastructure, hire. Without naming one, the firm stays in “we should think about this”.

WRITE THIS DOWN

If the firm decided this week to take AI seriously, what would the first move be? Why that one, and not the others?

“We should think about this” is not a move.

How would we do it?AUDIT16 / 30

Where it stalled

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Stalls get attributed to the last visible excuse. The actual blocker is usually a person or a permission nobody wants to name.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Where efforts to address this have stalled, and the actual blocker: partner buy-in, IT capacity, competing priorities, lack of clarity, lack of internal authority, something else.

Name the blocker, or the blocker wins.

How would we do it?MOVE17 / 30

One move, one owner, one date

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Firms adopt AI as a mood, not a project. Without one named move, nothing has a start date, so nothing starts.

THE MOVE

From the audit, pick the single highest-leverage first move. One owner, one date, thirty days to evidence.

One move with a date beats five moves in a memo.

Are we ready?AUDIT18 / 30

Where the knowledge lives

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Firms run for years without a map of where their written knowledge lives, because everyone who needs a document asks the person who has it.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Where the firm's important written knowledge lives — document management, shared drives, email, individuals' inboxes, paper. Is it organised, searchable, and accessible to the staff who need it?

If it lives in one person's inbox, it lives at their pleasure.

Are we ready?AUDIT19 / 30

The integration map

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Practice systems get chosen for billing, then quietly become the ceiling on everything else. Nobody asks about integrations until something needs one.

WRITE THIS DOWN

The technology AI tools would need to integrate with — practice management, document management, accounting, billing — and any of them locked or constrained in ways that would limit it.

The system you can't integrate with sets your ceiling.

Are we ready?AUDIT20 / 30

The local option

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Sovereign hosting sounds like an enterprise question. For regulated firms it is the question — and “we'd ask someone” is not a plan.

WRITE THIS DOWN

If the firm wanted sensitive matters in AI workflows without data leaving the building: is there anyone, internally or in its trusted network, who could evaluate or implement local AI infrastructure? If not, what's the plan?

“We'd ask someone” is not a plan.

Are we ready?AUDIT21 / 30

The capacity check

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Change capacity is assumed to exist because the change feels urgent. Teams already at capacity absorb nothing, and the initiative quietly dies.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Whether the firm has the people-time to absorb a meaningful change in how it works with AI in the next six to twelve months — or whether the team is already at capacity for any new initiative.

No spare hours, no adoption. Count them first.

Are we ready?MOVE22 / 30

Ask the lock question

WHY IT GOES WRONG

Vendors answer integration questions with brochures, and firms discover the constraint after signing, when the lock is already paid for.

THE MOVE

Ask each critical vendor three questions: can other systems read our data, can they write, and what does that cost? The answers go in the audit file.

Ask before the quote, not after the demo.

What's the value?AUDIT23 / 30

Concrete change

WHY IT GETS DODGED

“Efficiency” is the shape value takes in press releases. In firms it arrives as specific hours on specific documents.

WRITE THIS DOWN

If the firm closed its AI literacy gap in the next six months, what changes in concrete terms? Time saved on what; quality improved on what; risk reduced on what?

“On what?” is the whole question.

What's the value?AUDIT24 / 30

The policy's value

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Policies get valued as compliance objects, so their business case reads weak. The value is in what the firm can finally say and do.

WRITE THIS DOWN

If the firm wrote and operated a real AI use policy in the next twelve months, what would be the value? Be specific about the value, not the policy itself.

Sell the value, not the document.

What's the value?AUDIT25 / 30

The client questionnaire

WHY IT GETS DODGED

The questionnaire is hypothetical until it isn't — and by then, the answer is worth a tender, not a paragraph.

WRITE THIS DOWN

If a client questionnaire arrived next month asking how the firm handles AI in client material: what is the current answer worth? What would a confident answer be worth?

The answer has a price. So does the pause before it.

What's the value?AUDIT26 / 30

The cost of inaction

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Business cases get written for action, because action has a quote attached. Inaction feels free, so its cost is never written.

WRITE THIS DOWN

What it would cost the firm, in time and money, not to act on this in the next twelve months.

The right business case is the cost of inaction.

What's the value?AUDIT27 / 30

The honest range

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Partners hold different invisible numbers. Until they're said out loud, every proposal lands as a surprise.

WRITE THIS DOWN

What investment range the firm would consider if the case were clear. Tens of thousands? Six figures? Be honest about what the firm would and would not approve.

An unspoken range vetoes every plan.

What's the value?MOVE28 / 30

The cost-of-inaction memo

WHY IT GOES WRONG

AI proposals get judged against other spending, while standing still gets judged against nothing.

THE MOVE

Write the one-page cost of not acting, in the firm's own terms, and put it beside the investment range before any decision is made.

Standing still has an invoice too.

READ ME LASTAUDIT29 / 30

What your answers tell you

WHY IT GETS DODGED

Most firms read diagnostics hoping for a score. There is no score here. There are four states, and each points at different work.

WRITE THIS DOWN

Could answer most, confidently and specifically? The work is consolidation. Around half vague or generic? The most common state: literacy first, policy second. Most uncertain, several surprises? Begin with a structured conversation. Wanted to discuss the answers? That signal is the work.

No number at the end. The work is in the answering.
