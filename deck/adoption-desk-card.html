The Adoption Deck · Desk card

Five questions. Answered in writing.

The whole deck for partners and principals. Pin it up; work one section per partners' meeting, pen in hand.

01Can we do AI?

Capability and current state.

Tools: every tool the firm pays for, plus staff personal accounts · Uses: who, how often, for what — three tools, named · People: habitual users, non-users, and what the third group is doing · Shadow use: where AI is at work unseen · Support: sustained, or tolerated?

The move

Name the internal owner — the person staff ask "is this appropriate?" — and tell the firm.

02Should we do AI?

Opportunity and risk.

Value: not "drafting" — which drafts, reviewed by whom · Risk: name the workflow, not the category · Sensitive material: week, month, year — can you say what has passed through a tool? · Regulator: the plain-English answer, written and read back · Clients: who has asked, what did you say?

The move

One page, one position — what staff may do, what needs asking first, who owns the question. Dated. Reviewed twice a year.

03How would we do it?

Implementation orientation.

Training: what changed afterwards is the only part that counts · Policy: a policy nobody operates is a wish with a date on it · First move: workshop, policy, infrastructure, hire — name one · Stalls: name the blocker, or the blocker wins.

The move

One move, one owner, one date — thirty days to evidence.

04Are we ready?

Readiness check.

Knowledge: where it lives, organised, searchable, reachable · Integrations: the systems AI would need to touch, and the locks · Local option: who could evaluate sovereign infrastructure? · Capacity: people-time for a meaningful change in six to twelve months.

The move

Ask the lock question — read, write, and what it costs. Before the quote, not after the demo.

05What's the value?

Business case framing — in the firm's own terms.

Concrete change: time saved on what; quality improved on what; risk reduced on what · Policy value: what the firm can finally say and do · Questionnaire: the confident answer has a price.

Inaction: the right business case is the cost of standing still · Range: an unspoken range vetoes every plan · The move: the one-page cost-of-inaction memo, beside the range, before any decision.

Write, don't think

Thirty to sixty minutes, pen in hand, one answer per card. Written answers expose gaps that mental ones do not.

One section at a time

Work the sections per partners' meeting, not in one heroic sitting. The answers accumulate into the firm's position.

The four states

Could answer most: consolidate. Half vague: literacy first. Most uncertain: structured conversation, before any policy. Wanted to discuss: that signal is the work.
