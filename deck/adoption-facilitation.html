The Adoption Deck · Facilitation

Run it as a sixty-minute session.

For a partners' or leadership meeting. Three to eight people around one table, pens in hand. One session now; the rest of the deck one section per meeting after.

What you need on the table

The one-page guide, printed per person · the deck, sorted by section, within reach · one sheet per person for written answers · a visible timer · nothing else. Laptops closed.

TimeMove
0:00Frame it. One minute: this is not a decision meeting and not a policy meeting. It is a writing meeting. The point is to surface what the firm can and cannot currently answer. There is no score.
0:05Run the fifteen checks. Everyone silently works the one-page guide, marking yes or not yet for the firm. Eight minutes, alone. Compare only the counts, not the answers.
0:15Name the thinnest section. Count "not yet"s per section, out loud. The thinnest section becomes this session's work. If two tie, the one with the most partner disagreement wins.
0:20Write the audits. Work that section's audit cards one at a time. Two minutes per card, writing alone; then sixty seconds each reading your answer aloud. No debate while answers are read — the reader keeps their words.
0:40Run the move card. Take the section's move card and execute its first step in the room: name the owner, draft the page's first line, or write the cost. What can be finished now, finishes now.
0:50Book the next section. Date the next meeting and name its section now, while the room is warm. The deck travels: whoever owns the move keeps the cards.
0:57Close on card 29. Read "What your answers tell you" and let each person say which state the firm is in. If the room disagrees on the state, that disagreement is the finding — write it down.

If the writing is thin

Ask "would you say that sentence to the regulator?" If not, the answer isn't finished. One more pass, alone.

If partners disagree

Good. Disagreement about the facts is the session working. Record both answers verbatim; they reconcile later, on paper.

The one rule

Answers stay in the room and in writing. Nothing goes to staff, to clients, or to email until it is a position the firm has chosen on purpose.
